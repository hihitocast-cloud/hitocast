About HitoCast

About HitoCast

HitoCast is a small project with a simple idea: history is the best English teacher — if it is told like a story.

HitoCastLearn English through history

It started with salt

A few years ago, one of us watched a documentary about salt. Salt built empires. Salt started wars. Salt paid Roman soldiers — that is where the word salary comes from. It was history. But it felt like a story.

And a question would not go away: what if English learners could feel that? What if the history of ordinary things — the bread on the table, the salt in the kitchen, the coffee in the morning cup — could teach English the way a good story does, one word at a time? That question became HitoCast.

What HitoCast is

HitoCast is a small research team. We study the history of things that shaped the world — food, inventions, people, empires — and retell those stories in simple English for learners at A2 to B2 level. No textbooks. No lists of words to memorise. Just stories, told clearly, where the words you need arrive inside the story itself.

Every story starts as a video on our YouTube channel, where you can hear it told out loud. The full story lives here on the website, to read at your own pace, as many times as you like.

How to learn with HitoCast

Start with the video. Hear the story first, at a pace made for learners — clear voices, real rhythm, no rush. Then read the full story here. Same story, second pass: the words start to stick. And as you read, notice the words history hands you. Every story hides small gifts — the origins of everyday words, idioms, and phrases you will remember because the story carries them. The word salary, for example, is still in the Salt story, waiting where it has always been.

What you will find here

So far, HitoCast has ten stories. In Food History, you can follow bread from burned crumbs in Jordan to the bakeries of Paris, see how salt built empires, and learn how coffee travelled from the highlands of Ethiopia to two billion cups a day. In Inventions & Discoveries, there is the toilet — the invention that saved more lives than any doctor in history. In People, there is the woman who stayed: the complete history of nursing. And in Civilizations & Empires, there is the entire history of the United States, told as one long story.

Start anywhere. A good place is The History of Bread, The History of Salt, or The Dark and Beautiful History of Coffee — or browse all stories on the home page.

Who it is for

HitoCast is made for English learners at A2 to B2 level — and for anyone who is curious. You do not need to know history to enjoy it. You only need to like a good story. If you can follow a story, the English follows.

History is not boring. It was only ever badly told. Come and hear it told properly — watch a video, read a story, and pick up real English along the way.
